Privacy

Last updated 1 August 2026

Play the free road with no account, no email and no network. If you do, the only thing that leaves your device is an anonymous record of which questions you saw and how they went — never your name, never your email address.

Run by have you seen my quant. This is a static website: there is no server of ours anywhere. Everything below is what the code in your browser does, and all of it is readable in the page source.

Usage events

As you play, short events go to a hosted database (Supabase), so it is possible to see which questions are too hard and where people stop. Each one carries a random session id (new per tab session), a random device id kept in this browser, a visit count, the event name and time, and the details of that event: which question or lesson, whether the answer was right, how long it took, hearts, XP, streak, the screen you were on, your window size, the referring page, and a utm_source tag if your link had one.

Neither id is a name or tied to you anywhere; they exist so ten events from one visit count as one visit. Clearing this site's storage throws the device id away and you become a new, unrelated device.

Your email address is never put in an event. The email screen records only the domain after the @ and how many characters the address was. The address itself is not in the event stream and not in the events table. One thing to be honest about: a domain can identify you if it is your own — you@yourname.com leaves yourname.com in the log.

The events table is write-only from the browser: the key shipped in the page can add an event and nothing else — it cannot read the stream back, or read anybody's account. Only the owner can, through the database's own admin login.

Every event carries a host stamp, the hostname the page was served from. And analytics does not fire at all from file://, localhost, 127.0.0.1 or a local network address — that exists because the owner's own testing was otherwise indistinguishable from real visitors, but the effect is that development traffic is never recorded.

To see exactly what is recorded about you, add ?debug=1 to the address, or tap the small grey line at the bottom of the road three times. A drawer opens with this device's recent events, to read, copy or clear.

What stays in your browser

Your progress lives in this browser's localStorage: solved questions, attempts, XP, crowns, streak, daily goal, XP per day, and an email address if you entered one. Also there: the recent-events buffer, your device id, your sign-in session, and your membership state.

None of it is uploaded unless you sign in. Signed out, it stays on the device, and clearing the site's data in your browser erases it permanently — there is no copy to restore from.

Signing in by email

Past the free road the site asks for an email address. If you give one it is sent to Supabase, which emails you a one-time sign-in link. So for sign-in, and only for sign-in, the real address is sent and stored — in Supabase's own auth.users table, which is what makes the link work, and in a profiles row holding your address, XP, streak, best streak, last active day, daily goal, lesson crowns, the last 30 days of daily XP, and your membership state.

Once signed in, solved questions are also mirrored to a progress table — one row per question, with attempts, time spent and when you solved it. That is what lets you carry on from your phone on a laptop, and it is why the site asks at all. The database rules only ever let you read and write your own rows.

Opening the sign-in link wipes the tokens out of the address bar before anything else happens, so a link you paste to someone later is not a live session. Signing out deletes the session here and asks Supabase to revoke it. If the mail service cannot be reached, the site says so plainly, keeps your address on the device only, and opens the road anyway.

The membership, and Gumroad

The optional membership is sold and billed by Gumroad. Payment happens entirely on Gumroad's checkout: card details never touch this site and are never seen by us. What Gumroad collects there is theirs, under their privacy policy. Their checkout script loads only when you tap a buy button — until then the free road has no third-party code on it at all.

Gumroad issues a licence key. Pasting it in is how the site learns you are a member, and it is used for exactly one thing: asking Gumroad's public licence-check endpoint, about once a day, whether that subscription is still live. The key is kept on your device, and on your profile row if you are signed in, with the membership status, the date access runs to, when it was last checked, and the Gumroad subscription id. It is kept so the re-check need not ask you again, so one key stays with one account, and so a new phone is "paste the key" rather than an email to a human.

Hosting

The site is static files served by GitHub Pages. There is no application server and no logging of ours. GitHub serves every request, so GitHub sees the ordinary web-server record of it — your IP address, the page asked for, the time. That is GitHub's, under GitHub's privacy statement; we neither see it nor control it. The same is unavoidably true of Supabase and Gumroad: a request carries your IP to whoever answers it.

Our Instagram account, and Meta's API

Separately from this website, we run the Instagram account @have_you_seen_my_quant, and we use a Meta app of the same name to post to it. That app acts on our own account and nobody else's: it uploads our videos, writes the first comment under them, and reads back the view and reach counts for the posts we have made, so we can tell which puzzles land. No other Instagram account can connect to it.

If you comment on one of our posts, the comment is already public on Instagram and is Meta's, under Meta's own privacy policy. Where our app can see comments on our posts, it uses them only to reply to the person who wrote one. We do not build profiles of commenters, we do not export follower lists, and we do not join anything from Instagram to anything on this site — an Instagram username is never matched to an email address or to a session here. The only thing that crosses over is a utm_source tag on a link, which says a visit came from Instagram and nothing about who you are.

No advertising, no tracking pixels

None. The site references exactly three outside addresses, and this is all of them: *.supabase.co (events, sign-in, sync), gumroad.com (checkout, on tap only) and api.gumroad.com (the licence check). No ad network, no analytics SDK, no social pixel, no web fonts, no CDN, no embedded video, and no cookies set by this site. Nothing is sold or handed to anyone.

It is a site about interview maths, made for adults. Nothing on it is directed at children.

Deleting your data

  • On your device, now. Clear this site's data in your browser. Progress, events, session and membership all go, with no copy left.
  • Your account. Email Haveyouseenmyquant@gmail.com from the address you signed in with. That removes the account, the profile row and every progress row attached to it.
  • Usage events. They are not stored against a person, so there is nothing to look you up by. If you want yours gone anyway, open the debug drawer above, copy your device id and send it — those rows can then be found and deleted.

The same address is the right one for any question about any of this, or to ask what is held about you.

If what the site does changes, this page changes with it and the date at the top moves. This describes the practice; it is not a certification against any particular regulation.

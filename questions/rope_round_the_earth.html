Solve this: add one metre around Earth. How big is the gap?

A rope starts tight around the equator. Add exactly one metre, then lift it evenly all the way round. The question is not how long the rope is; it is how much radius that extra metre buys.

The same move around a tennis ball gives the same gap. The original circle size disappears when you compare the before and after circumferences; only the added metre is left.

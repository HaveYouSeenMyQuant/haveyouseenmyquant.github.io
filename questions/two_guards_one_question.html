Two guards, one question

puzzlesposted 6 Aug 2026

Two doors, two guards, one of them always lies. One question.

One door leads out. The other does not. A guard stands at each: one answers every question honestly, the other lies to every question, and both of them know which door is which.

You do not know which guard is which. You may ask ONE guard ONE yes-or-no question, and then you must choose a door.

The hard part is not that a guard might lie to you. It is that you cannot tell which one you are speaking to, so any straightforward question gives you an answer you have no way to grade. Ask 'does this door lead out?' and yes means opposite things depending on who said it - and it fails exactly half the time, which is the same as not asking.

So you need a question whose answer comes out the SAME whichever guard happens to hear it. That sounds impossible, and it is not.

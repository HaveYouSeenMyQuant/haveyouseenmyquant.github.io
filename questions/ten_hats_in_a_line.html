Ten people in a line, a black or white hat each — how many are saved?

puzzlesposted 30 Jul 2026

Ten people in a line. Each gets a black or white hat. You only see the hats in front. How many can be saved?

Read the rules carefully, because ambiguity is what makes people argue about this one.

Everyone faces the same way. You see every hat ahead of you in the line and nothing else — never your own, never the ones behind you.

Starting at the very back and moving forward, each person says exactly one word out loud: "black" or "white". Everyone hears every word. Say your own hat colour correctly and you are saved.

The hats are placed by someone who knows your plan. You may agree that plan in advance, but once the hats are on, the one word is all you get — no pauses, no tone, no gestures, no delays.

And it has to work for every possible arrangement of the ten hats, not a lucky one.

Nobody can see their own hat, so it feels like half of them are guessing. Far more than half can be saved for certain.

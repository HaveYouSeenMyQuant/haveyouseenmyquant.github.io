Twelve coins, one is the wrong weight, three weighings

puzzlesposted 2 Aug 2026

Twelve coins, one is the wrong weight, and nobody tells you which way. Solve it in three weighings.

The setup in full. Twelve coins that look and feel identical. Exactly one of them weighs differently from the other eleven — and you are not told whether it is heavier or lighter. Your only tool is a balance: two pans, and it answers one of three things. This side down, that side down, or dead level. No numbers, no readings.

Three weighings. At the end you must name the odd coin AND say whether it is heavy or light. Not "probably" — every time, whichever coin it is and whichever way it went.

Here is why three is such an interesting number to be given. Twelve coins, either direction, is twenty-four different things that could be true. Each weighing has three outcomes, so three of them can tell twenty-seven stories apart. Twenty-four fits inside twenty-seven with three slots to spare. Two weighings give nine, and nine is less than twenty-four, so two can never work for any strategy whatsoever. There is almost no slack in this puzzle, and that is what makes it hard: waste a little information anywhere and you run out.

That tightness even forces the first move. Six against six leaves twelve possibilities the moment it tips. Three against three leaves twelve when it comes out level. Only four against four splits the twenty-four into eight, eight and eight — small enough for the two weighings you have left. Which is exactly what the video does, and then stops.

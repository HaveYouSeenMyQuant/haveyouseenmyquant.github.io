Two coins are separated by evidence, not by vote count

information_theoryposted 3 Aug 2026

Two coins can be close in heads and far in evidence.

One coin is fair. The other lands heads ninety percent of the time. You see a long run from one coin, but you are not told which coin made it.

A head is evidence for the ninety percent coin, but only a small amount, because the fair coin still makes heads half the time. A tail is much stronger evidence for the fair coin, because the ninety percent coin almost never makes tails.

That running likelihood score is the useful idea. The distance between two distributions is the average speed of that score, and the speed changes depending on which coin is actually generating the flips.

One linear map, one area multiplier

linear_algebraposted 2 Aug 2026

A slanted grid changes every area by one secret number. Answer's in bio — free, takes an email.

A linear map moves the whole plane with one rule. One unit square becomes one slanted tile. A far-away unit square becomes the same slanted tile, just shifted.

That is the key: shifting a shape does not change its area. So a bigger shape made from lots of tiny squares is scaled by the same factor, tile by tile.

For this map, the factor is the area of that slanted tile compared with the original unit square. That factor has a name: the determinant. The video stops before the number.

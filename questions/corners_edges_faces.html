Corners, edges, faces — what never changes

geometryposted 8 Aug 2026

Count a solid's corners, edges and faces. Something never changes.

A tetrahedron: 4 corners, 6 edges, 4 faces. A cube: 8, 12 and 6. A football: 60, 90 and 32.

Nothing about those numbers looks related. Combine them the right way and every single solid gives the same answer - a pyramid, a prism, a football, something you invent this afternoon.

It does not care about lengths, or angles, or whether the faces are regular, or how you draw it. Only about how the pieces connect.

That last part is what makes it interesting rather than a curiosity. It is the first properly topological fact most people meet: a number that survives any amount of stretching and bending.

And when it comes out WRONG, that is more informative still - what it is really counting is something about the shape you would not think to look for.

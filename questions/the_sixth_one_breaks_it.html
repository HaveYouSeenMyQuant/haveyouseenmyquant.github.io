The sixth one breaks it

geometryposted 27 Aug 2026

1, 2, 4, 8, 16 — and then it is not 32.

Mark points on a circle and join every pair with a straight line. Count the regions the circle is cut into.

One point: 1. Two: 2. Then 4, 8, 16. Everybody supplies the next term without being asked.

It is 31.

Counted from the real arrangement rather than from a formula: every chord crossing found numerically, the planar graph assembled, and Euler's V−E+F=2 applied — then compared with the closed form. Two routes that could disagree, and do not.

Five confirmations of a pattern are not a proof.

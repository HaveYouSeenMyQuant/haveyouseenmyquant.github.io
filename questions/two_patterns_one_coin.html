Two patterns, one coin

probabilityposted 5 Aug 2026

A fair coin. You wait for HH, they wait for HT.

Flip a fair coin over and over. You stop the moment you see two heads in a row. Somebody else stops the moment they see a head followed by a tail.

Now look at any two flips you like - flips 7 and 8, say. The chance they are HH is a quarter. The chance they are HT is a quarter. The two patterns are equally likely, everywhere, always.

So you would both wait the same number of flips on average. That is the obvious answer, almost everyone gives it, and it is wrong.

The patterns are equally likely. What they are not is equally easy to reach - and the difference is in what happens to you when the next flip is the wrong one.

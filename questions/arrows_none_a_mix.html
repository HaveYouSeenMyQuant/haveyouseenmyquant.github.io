Arrows that aren't mixes of each other.

Draw two arrows from a point on a flat page. Now every direction you can point is just so much of the first plus so much of the second - including backwards, using negative amounts.

So draw a third arrow anywhere you like. It is not new. It was already reachable from the two you had, and you can work out exactly how much of each it takes.

The question is how many arrows you can draw on that page so that NONE of them is a mix of the others - and then what the same question answers in three dimensions, and in a hundred.

What does a derivative actually measure?

calculusposted 3 Aug 2026

The two points meet, but the line survives.

A derivative is not a formula first. It is a line trying to survive a collapse.

Start with two points on a curve. The straight line through them gives the average climb from the first point to the second. Then move the second point closer. The average is now measured across a smaller piece of the same curve.

As the gap shrinks, the two points become almost one point. The surprising part is that the line does not become meaningless. For a smooth curve, its direction settles. That surviving direction is the tangent, and its steepness is the derivative at the marked point.

Three slot rings hide the Chinese remainder theorem

number_theoryposted 3 Aug 2026

Two slot rings hide a count.

Start with a 3-slot ring and a 5-slot ring. One slot on each is painted gold, and both gold slots start at the top. Each tick moves each ring by one of its own slots.

So after k ticks, the 3-slot ring shows where k lands after dividing by 3. The 5-slot ring does the same for dividing by 5. The pair of positions is a fingerprint for the tick count until the machine finally repeats.

Then add a 7-slot ring. Now you have three remainders at once. Because 3, 5, and 7 do not share a rhythm, the triple pins down the count until the whole machine comes back.

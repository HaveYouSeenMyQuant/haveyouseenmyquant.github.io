Three switches, one bulb behind a closed door, one look

puzzlesposted 2 Aug 2026

Three switches, one bulb behind a closed door — and you only get to look inside once. Try it yourself.

Most people call it impossible in about four seconds. It isn't.

Here is the whole setup, because the constraints ARE the puzzle.

Three ordinary light switches are on the wall outside a closed room. Inside hangs a single bulb. Exactly one of the three switches controls it; the other two are wired to nothing at all. The room is sealed — no window, no gap under the door — so with the door shut you cannot tell whether the bulb is lit.

You may flip the switches as much as you like, in any order, and wait as long as you like. Then you open the door. Once. You look, the door shuts, and you have to name the switch.

WITH TWO LOOKS IT'S TRIVIAL. Flip switch one, open the door: if the bulb is on, done; if it's off, that switch is out. Flip switch two, open again, and either way you know. Two readings, three possibilities, no cleverness required.

WITH ONE LOOK IT LOOKS LIKE A COUNTING IMPOSSIBILITY, and this is the part worth sitting with. There are exactly three possible worlds: switch one is live, switch two is live, switch three is live. You get one reading. If the only thing that reading can come back with is "lit" or "not lit", that's two outcomes trying to separate three worlds — and no strategy on earth fixes that. Two boxes cannot hold three things. It isn't a hard problem at that point, it's a proved impossible one.

So either the puzzle is broken, or your one look can come away with more than two answers.

It isn't broken. Read the rules once more and notice which of the two freedoms you were handed you haven't spent. Nothing about "on or off" improves if you wait — so why does the puzzle go out of its way to give you unlimited time?

That's the whole hint you need. Most people who get it get it in the next thirty seconds.

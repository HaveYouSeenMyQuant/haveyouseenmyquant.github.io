Most of a high-dimensional ball is skin

ml_fundamentalsposted 8 Aug 2026

Shave the outer 1% off a ball. In 100 dimensions, how much came off?

Take a ball of radius 1 and remove the outermost one per cent of the radius - the thin shell between 0.99 and 1.

In two dimensions that peel is about 2% of the area. In three, about 3% of the volume. A skin, as you would expect.

Now ask it in a hundred dimensions. The peel is still one per cent of the radius - the same sliver, measured the same way.

This is the curse of dimensionality in its most concrete form, and it is why nearest neighbours stop being near, why a local sample stops being local, and why grid search dies above a handful of dimensions.

A sum that never stops

calculusposted 6 Aug 2026

A sum whose terms shrink to nothing, and which still passes every number you name.

Add a half, then a third, then a quarter, and keep going forever.

It passes 10 after exactly 12,367 terms — counted, not estimated. It reaches 14.4 after a million.

To reach 100 you need about 1.5 × 10^43 terms. More terms than there are atoms in the earth, for a total you could count to out loud.

And the check that makes it mean something: 1/n² has terms going to zero as well, and that one stops — at π²/6. Vanishing terms decide nothing.

The bad move that finds the better valley

optimizationposted 2 Aug 2026

The best move in this search is the one that looks wrong. Answer's in bio — free, takes an email.

A greedy search only accepts improvements. That sounds sensible, but on a bumpy landscape it can be fatal.

In the clip, both balls start from the same point. The left ball is greedy. It rolls downhill into the first dip, then every legal nudge from there goes uphill, so it refuses them all. It has zero chance of reaching the deeper valley on the right.

The right ball is doing the same local search with one extra rule: early on, while it is hot, it sometimes accepts a worse move. That is not a bug. Those worse moves are the only way to climb out of a shallow trap and cross the ridge.

Cooling matters because you cannot stay reckless forever. If bad moves keep passing at the end, the ball can wander straight back out. Annealing starts loose enough to escape, then becomes strict enough to settle.

For this exact toy landscape, the greedy process reaches the deep basin 0% of the time. The annealed process, with the proposal rule drawn here, reaches it about 58% of the time.

The sample's spread runs small

statisticsposted 6 Aug 2026

Measure a sample's spread the obvious way and it comes out too small. Always.

You have a handful of measurements. Find their average, see how far each one sits from it, square those distances, average them. That is the spread.

It is too small. Not sometimes - on average, every single time.

And it is not noise you can average away. Repeat the experiment a million times and the answer settles firmly below the truth. Taking more SAMPLES never fixes it. Taking bigger ones does.

The amount it is short by depends on nothing except how many measurements you took. Not the distribution, not its shape, not how spread out it really is.

The cause is one word, and it is not the arithmetic: you measured the distances from a centre that was chosen to make them small.

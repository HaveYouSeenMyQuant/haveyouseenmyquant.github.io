Two ways to keep the dials small — only one switches a dial off

ml_fundamentalsposted 2 Aug 2026

Two ways to tell a model to keep its numbers small. One of them deletes a feature outright.

Same data. Same two coefficients. Same strength of squeeze. One rule ends up setting a coefficient to exactly zero — not 0.003, zero — and the other never does, however hard you squeeze.

The reason is geometry, not algebra, and the whole thing fits in one picture.

Fit two coefficients. One pair fits your data best, and around it the error contours are nested ellipses: the further out, the worse the fit. Now impose a budget. Cap the sum of the absolute values (L1, lasso) and the allowed region is a diamond. Cap the sum of the squares (L2, ridge) and it is a circle. Both centred on the origin, both shrinking as you tighten.

The constrained answer is the lowest-error point inside the region — exactly where the growing ellipse first touches the boundary.

Now look at the two boundaries. The diamond has corners, and its corners sit ON the axes, the lines where one coefficient is precisely zero. The circle has no corners anywhere; it turns smoothly through every direction.

So ask where a growing ellipse is likely to land. On a smooth boundary the touch has to be a tangency — one condition, one spot per ellipse. A corner has no single tangent to match, so it wins for a whole range of ellipses at once.

In the clip the budget tightens from 100% of the free-fit budget to 49%, and the second dial is still alive on both shapes. Keep going and one dies exactly, at a specific computable budget, while the other only ever gets smaller.

The shortest route is not the one with the fewest roads

graphsposted 2 Aug 2026

Solve this map before the wave reaches the finish.

A lot of people count roads. One road, two roads, three roads. That is the trap.

The numbers on the roads are travel times. A shortest-path search does not care how many roads you have used, and it does not care how long a road looks on the picture. It cares about the earliest time the wave can arrive at a town.

Start at S. The wave goes out along every road at once. The long-looking road to E has time 3, so E is confirmed before A, even though A looks closer. Then E sends a new wave into D, and D gets confirmed before some towns that look more central.

That is the whole rule: the next town is the one with the smallest confirmed arrival time. Once a town lights up, no later detour can beat that time, because every road time is positive. The search is not greedy about the next road. It is greedy about the next arrival.

Now use that rule to finish the map. T has more than one way to be reached, and the route that looks neat is not necessarily the one that arrives first.

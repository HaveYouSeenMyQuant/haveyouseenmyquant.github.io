Three ordinary exchange rates, one loop, and the money does not come back

financeposted 1 Aug 2026

Three ordinary exchange rates. Send a million dollars round the loop and it doesn't come back a million.

Here is the whole setup, and it needs no finance background at all.

An exchange rate is just what one unit of money buys of another. Three currencies, three rates, one per pair:

$1 buys €0.92 €1 buys £0.85 £1 buys $1.28

Read them side by side and they look completely ordinary. Nothing about any single one of them is strange.

Now start with a million dollars and walk the loop. Dollars into euros: €920,000. Euros into pounds: £782,000. Pounds back into dollars — and that is where the video stops.

What lands back on the pile? More than a million, or less?

Two things worth noticing before you answer. First, the pile visibly SHRANK on the first two legs, and that tells you nothing at all — the only thing that matters is what the three rates multiply to. Second, the loop has a direction: go round it the other way and every multiplication becomes a division, so whatever one direction does, the other undoes.

And the honest part, because this is a market and not a puzzle box. A gap like this is exactly what a room full of automated traders exists to hunt, which is why it isn't there: on real major currencies the three quotes multiply to one to within a whisker, all day, every day. The pinned comment works out what an actual bid-ask spread on all three legs does to it, and how big a spread it takes to eat the whole thing.

Why chain-rule rates multiply

calculusposted 5 Aug 2026

Three linked sliders turn one small move into a bigger one.

The setup is deliberately plain. Slider A drives slider B, and that first link doubles the movement. Slider B then drives slider C, and the second link triples the movement it receives.

The trap is to treat the two labels as separate facts. They are not separate once the machines are connected. The output of the first link is the input to the second one.

That is the chain rule in motion: a small change passes through one rate, then the result passes through the next rate. The video stops at the question: one unit into A means how much movement out of C?

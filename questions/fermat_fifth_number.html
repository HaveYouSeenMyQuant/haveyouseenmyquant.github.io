The sixth Fermat number

number_theoryposted 14 Aug 2026

3, 5, 17, 257, 65537 all prime. Is 4,294,967,297?

Take 2, raise it to a power of 2, add 1. You get 3, 5, 17, 257, 65537 - and every one of them is prime.

In 1640 Fermat conjectured the rule makes primes forever. He could not check the next one, and he tried for thirty years.

The next one is 4,294,967,297. To settle it by dividing you would need every prime up to 65,536 - that is 6,542 long divisions on a ten-digit number, by hand.

Ninety years later one man settled it in FIVE tries. Not by dividing faster. By proving first that any factor had to have a particular shape, which threw away 99.9% of the candidates before he did any arithmetic at all.

A ladder slips down a wall.

The foot slides out along the floor and the top slides down the wall. Both ends move the entire time, and they do not move at the same speed - near the end the top drops away much faster than the foot travels.

Now watch the middle of the ladder. It is being dragged by two things that are both accelerating differently, so its path should be some awkward curve.

It is not awkward at all, and the reason is one line of school geometry about right-angled triangles that most people never think to apply here.

The video stops before the reveal: what does the midpoint trace, and why does a point a third of the way along NOT do the same thing?

The shortest walk across the surface of a cube

puzzlesposted 1 Aug 2026

Everyone's route across a cube is too long. The best one crosses the edge at a spot nobody guesses.

2.24 cube-edges, not 3.

A dot sits on one corner of a cube. It has to reach the corner diagonally opposite, and it can only walk on the surface — no tunnelling through the middle.

Two routes come to mind straight away. Follow the edges: three whole sides, length 3. Or cut diagonally across one face and climb the last edge: √2 + 1 = 2.414. The second is shorter, so most people stop there.

Both are beaten, and the way to see it is to stop thinking in 3D. Take the two faces the walk uses and unfold them flat. Side by side they make a rectangle 2 long and 1 tall, and on a flat rectangle the shortest route between two corners is not a debate — it's the straight line. √(2² + 1²) = √5 = 2.2360679…

Now fold that rectangle back into a cube. The straight line bends around the shared edge, crossing it exactly halfway up. Not at a corner — dead centre. On the folded cube that looks like a kink with no reason to be where it is; the reason only shows up when the cube is flat.

In cube-edges: along the edges 3.000 · across a face and climb 2.414 · the unfolded straight line 2.236. The edge route is 34% longer than it needs to be.

Straight through the inside would be √3 = 1.732, but that's a hole, not a walk.

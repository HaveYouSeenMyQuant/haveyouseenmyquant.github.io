The credit card minimum payment that takes 28 years

everydayposted 1 Aug 2026

Your credit card's minimum payment gets smaller exactly as fast as your debt does.

£3,000 at 24.9% APR, paying only the minimum: 28 years, and £5,395 of interest.

Here is the mechanism, and it is startlingly clean. A UK minimum payment is 1% of what you owe plus that month's interest, with a £5 floor. So the card charges the interest, then takes that payment off:

balance + interest − (1% of balance + interest) = 0.99 × balance

The interest cancels. Your balance falls by exactly 1% a month — and that is true whatever the APR happens to be. The rate does not set the pace, it only sets the price.

0.99 to the power of anything never reaches zero. It takes 284 months just to drop below the £5 floor, then 57 more months of £5 to finish. 341 months. 28.4 years. £5,394.92 of interest on £3,000 borrowed, which is 1.8 times what you actually spent.

That is what a percentage minimum does: the payment chasing the debt gets weaker at precisely the rate the debt gets smaller. It is built never to catch up.

Now change one thing. Take the very first minimum payment — £86.10 — and refuse to let it fall. Same money, out of the same account, as month one. The debt is gone in 57 months. Four years and nine months, and £1,899.76 of interest.

Same first payment. 23.7 years of difference. The only thing that changed is that one of them was allowed to shrink.

Set up a standing order for the first minimum and forget the word "minimum" exists.

(Nothing new added to the card, no fees or missed payments — just the balance and the rule. Change the APR to 21.9% or 29.9% and it is still decades against a handful of years.)

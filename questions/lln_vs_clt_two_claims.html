Two coin-flip laws that people mash together

statisticsposted 4 Aug 2026

Two laws of coin flips get mashed together.

One fair coin is flipped again and again. The running average is one claim: it moves toward one half, and the ordinary wobble shrinks as more flips arrive.

The bell on the right is a different claim. It does not follow one run. It freezes the experiment at a fixed size, looks across many runs, and rescales the miss from one half so different sizes can be compared on the same axis.

That distinction is the point people blur. One statement says where the average goes. The other says the shape of the error left over after the correct rescaling.

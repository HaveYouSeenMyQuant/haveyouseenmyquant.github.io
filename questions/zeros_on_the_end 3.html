Zeros on the end of a hundred factorial

number_theoryposted 6 Aug 2026

100 factorial ends in a run of zeros. How many?

Multiply every whole number from 1 to 100 together. The result is 158 digits long and it finishes with a run of zeros.

How many zeros are on the end?

Nearly everyone who has a real go at this lands on the same number, by the same route, and it is not quite right. The route is sound. It just misses something, and what it misses is small enough that you can look straight at it and not notice.

A hint at the shape of it: a zero on the end has to be MADE by something inside the product. Work out what makes one, then count those - not the numbers that obviously contain them.

Worth knowing beyond the puzzle: it is the difference between counting multiples and counting factors, and it is the same slip people make all over combinatorics.

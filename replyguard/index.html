Replyguard

Someone comments your keyword. They get your link, in their DMs.

Creators who post questions get comments full of attempted answers. Replying to all of them by hand does not scale. Replying to them with an unsupervised language model is worse: it confirms wrong answers, gives away the punchline of a post that has not landed yet, and does it publicly under your name.

Replyguard puts a refusal layer between the drafter and Instagram. You tell it what this post is holding back. Every draft is checked against four tests before anything is posted, and a draft that fails is logged and dropped — never softened and retried.

The four tests

  • Does the reply carry a number that appears in the material you are protecting?
  • Does it affirm the commenter — "exactly", "spot on", "that's it"? Confirming correct working gives it away as surely as stating it.
  • Does it use a word that appears in the protected text but nowhere public on the post?
  • In strict mode: does it have a shape that can only be telling them something? A safe reply either asks them something back or points at where the full answer lives.

The fourth test is the one that matters. The first three are a blacklist, and on our own corpus of real failures a blacklist caught only half.
